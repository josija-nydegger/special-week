mBot Final Task – user guide

How to load, start, test and tune FinalTask/FinalTask.ino. The program runs the whole course, including the flashlight search and the bell in the top area.

1. What the robot does

  1. Count. Drives slowly over the start block and counts the white stripes (1–4), with a short beep per stripe, one step higher each time. It does not stop, but drives straight on.
  2. Branch. Follows the line into branch 1–4 (numbered from left to right).
  3. First bottle. Stops 8 cm in front of it and drives around it: paths 1 and 4 on the outside (table edge side), paths 2 and 3 on the inside (centre side).
  4. Second bottle. Follows the line straight across the middle junction to the other bottle, bumps it, reverses and turns around.
  5. To the box. Follows the line back, takes the exit at the middle junction, stops and centres itself on the line, then follows the S-curve that is not covered by paper. Stops 7 cm in front of the box. It is then still in the S-curve and stands crooked in front of the box.
  6. Box. Sweeps the ultrasonic sensor across the box face and turns itself exactly square to the box. How crooked it stood tells it the side: turned left = box on the left line, turned right = right line. Then it reverses round the box on the side facing the middle of the course, pushes it towards the table edge until its line sensors find the line again, turns onto the line and drives through the passage. Stops there with a short melody.
  7. Flashlight and bell. Follows the line to its end, turns once on the spot and measures the light: the brightest direction is the flashlight. Drives towards it in 15 cm legs, checking the direction after each leg. About 22 cm before the wall it squares up to the wall with the ultrasonic sensor, checks the light once more and side-steps along the wall until the light is straight ahead. Then it drives in to about 10 cm, measures the wall once more and drives the rest blind until its front (the ultrasonic sensor) is 2 cm from the wall – the bell hangs about 3 cm in front of the wall, so the robot touches it before the wall. Backs off 5 cm and plays the melody.

How it picks the branch

Up to the first bottle the robot works like PreTask.ino: it follows the middle of the line, notices a T-junction because the line ends in front of it, drives on a little and turns on the spot to the wanted side until it sees the line again.

After the first bottle it follows one edge of the line (one sensor on white, one on black). On the way to the second bottle it follows the edge away from the exit and so passes the middle junction straight. On the way back the same edge leads into the exit.

2. Load the program

Robot setup

  • mBot with mCore board. Line follower on port 2, ultrasonic sensor on port 3, left motor on M1, right motor on M2 (the standard build). Other ports: change PORT_LINE / PORT_ULTRA.
  • Fresh or fully charged batteries. All timed moves depend on the battery voltage, so calibrate and run with the same charge level.

Upload

  • Arduino IDE: install the Makeblock library (GitHub: Makeblock-official/Makeblock-Libraries, add it via Sketch → Include Library → Add .ZIP Library). Open FinalTask/FinalTask.ino, choose board Arduino Uno and the mBot's serial port, then upload.
  • mBlock 5: add the mBot device, switch to Upload mode, open the Arduino C editor, paste the complete content of FinalTask.ino and upload.

To change a parameter: edit the value in section 1. PARAMETERS at the top of the file and upload again.

3. Run it

  1. Set RUN_MODE = MODE_FULL, FORCE_PATH = 0, BOX_SIDE = SIDE_AUTO.
  2. Switch the robot on. The LEDs turn blue: it is waiting.
  3. Place it on the black start block, pointing along the line, with both line sensors on black, behind the first white stripe. Use the same position as with PreTask.ino.
  4. Press the on-board button. After a short beep and 0.6 s it starts.
  5. Emergency stop: press the button again at any time. The robot stops and goes back to waiting (blue LEDs).

5. Test step by step

Work through the tests in this order. Each one checks one part, so you never have to run the whole course to tune one number. Set RUN_MODE, upload, place the robot, press the button.

Test 1 – sensors (MODE_SENSORS)

Nothing moves. Open the serial monitor at 115200 baud. Slide the robot over the line by hand.

  • The left value must say BLACK when the left sensor (seen in driving direction) is over the line. If it is the wrong way round, set SWAP_LINE_SENSORS = true.
  • Hold a bottle in front of the robot: the distance must match a ruler within about 1 cm between 4 and 30 cm.
  • Slide the robot over the white stripes: both sensors must show white on every stripe.

Test 2 – straight calibration (MODE_CAL_STRAIGHT)

Drives 1 s at speed 100. Measure the distance and write it into CAL_CM_PER_SEC (now 23). If a wheel turns backwards, flip LEFT_MOTOR_SIGN or RIGHT_MOTOR_SIGN. If the robot drifts to one side, lower the trim of the faster wheel a little (for example RIGHT_TRIM = 0.96).

Test 3 – turn calibration (MODE_CAL_TURN)

Turns 90° left, waits, turns 90° right. Put the robot along a line on the floor to judge it. Turns too far: lower CAL_TURN_MS_90 (now 650). Not far enough: raise it. This value matters most for the box manoeuvre, which has four turns in a row.

Test 4 – counting (MODE_COUNT)

Start on the start block. You hear one short beep per stripe (and see a white flash) while it drives over them, then it stops. Count the beeps: it must be correct for 1, 2, 3 and 4 stripes, several times each. If a stripe is suddenly missed since the stripe beeps were added, set STRIPE_BEEP = false and tell us.

Test 4b – way to the first bottle (MODE_PRETASK)

Start on the start block. The robot counts (one beep per stripe), drives on without stopping to the matching branch, stops about 5 cm in front of the first bottle (PRETASK_STOP_CM) and finishes (green). Same job as PreTask.ino.

Test 5 – around the bottle (MODE_AVOID)

Set FORCE_PATH to the path you test. Place the robot on that branch about 30 cm below the bottle, on the line. It follows the branch, stops in front of the bottle, drives around it, finds the line and stops 15 cm later. On paths 2 and 3 it must end up pointing away from the bottle, towards the middle junction. Test all four paths. Tune with the table in section 7.

Test 6 – second bottle and way to the box (MODE_SECOND)

Set FORCE_PATH (1 or 2 = start behind the left bottle, 3 or 4 = behind the right bottle). Place the robot on the line just behind the first bottle, pointing away from it. It must drive straight across the middle junction, bump the second bottle, turn around, take the exit at the junction, and stop in front of the box with the correct left/right signal. Repeat with the paper on the other line.

Test 6b – middle junction (MODE_JUNCTION)

Set FORCE_PATH (1 or 2 = come from the right, 3 or 4 = come from the left). Place the robot on the horizontal cross line about 15 cm before the middle junction, pointing towards it. It must turn into the exit, turn cyan, stop, make small turns left and right to centre on the line, then do exactly what MODE_BOX does. This tests the part between the junction and the S many times quickly.

Test 6c – squaring up to the box (MODE_SQUARE)

This is the basis of the new box part, so test it first. Place the robot about 8 cm in front of the box, pointing at it but turned 20–30° to one side, and press the button. It turns left, sweeps right across the box and turns back.

  • It must end up pointing straight at the box (square to its front). Still turned a little to the right: raise SCAN_TRIM_MS by 20. To the left: lower it.
  • The LEDs then show how it stood before: blue + 1 beep = it was turned to the left, yellow + 2 beeps = to the right, white = about straight. This must match how you placed it.
  • Try both sides, 10°, 20° and 35°, and also with the robot standing in front of the left and the right third of the box.
  • The serial monitor prints the angle and the distance.

Test 7 – box (MODE_BOX)

Place the robot on the single line between the middle junction and the fork, pointing towards the box. It drives to the box, pushes it and drives through. Test the left and the right line.

Test 9 – light direction (MODE_LIGHT_SCAN)

Place the robot anywhere in the top area, flashlight on and pointing roughly at it. It turns once, then turns to the brightest direction: green + two beeps. Red + low beep = no clear direction (contrast below LIGHT_MIN_CONTRAST). It must end up pointing at the flashlight. Always a bit to the right of it: raise LIGHT_DIR_TRIM_DEG (e.g. 10); to the left: lower it. The serial monitor prints the light every 10°.

Test 10 – flashlight and bell (MODE_LIGHT)

Place the robot on the line after the box (it follows it to the end first) or anywhere in the top area without a line (it starts searching at once). Try the bell on the back wall, on a side wall and near a corner. Watch:

  • It must never touch a wall. If it gets too close on the final drive in: raise BELL_GAP_CM. If it stops before reaching the bell: lower it. Test 11 tunes this without the flashlight.
  • The default assumes the bell hangs about 3 cm in front of the wall (estimated from photos). Check it at the test runs and adjust BELL_GAP_CM.
  • Side-step wrong way: the light direction is off – check test 9 first.

Test 11 – drive in to the bell (MODE_BELL)

Works against any wall, no flashlight needed – good for the test runs. Place the robot 15–35 cm in front of a wall, roughly facing it. It squares up, drives in and stops with its front BELL_GAP_CM from the wall, waits 0.5 s, backs off. Measure the gap with a ruler while it waits, several times. With a bell: it must ring every time and the wall must never be touched. The serial monitor shows whether it used the ultrasonic or the driven distance for the last part.

Test 8 – full run with a forced path

MODE_FULL with FORCE_PATH = 1…4 runs the whole course on a chosen path, whatever the stripes say. Finish with FORCE_PATH = 0.

5b. Light sensor test (LightTest/LightTest.ino)

A separate small program to find out what the built-in light sensor (on top of the mCore board, looking up) can do with the flashlight. Choose TEST_MODE at the top, upload, press the button.

8. Known limits

  • Clearances are small. From the PDF: bottle centre to table edge is about 26 cm, bottle centre to centre beam about 21 cm, and the robot is 13 cm wide. The inside pass (paths 2 and 3) leaves roughly 2 cm on each side if a beam really stands between the bottles. Tune test 5 carefully for those two paths.
  • Blind moves drift. Around the bottle and around the box the robot drives by time only. Battery level, floor and wheel slip change the result.
  • The box is pushed towards the table edge and ends about 6 cm from it. The robot goes round the box on the side facing the middle of the course, where there is room. If the side decision is wrong, it goes round the outer side instead and, on the real table, would come within about 1 cm of the table edge.
  • Room around the box on the real table (from the PDF): beams 29 cm in front of the box and 17 cm behind its front edge. The new moves are planned to keep 2–4 cm away from both, but they are timed moves and cannot be tested on the practice course without beams. Put two boards or books at those distances to test it.
  • The side decision relies on the ultrasonic sweep. It assumes the reading is smallest when the robot points straight at the box. That is checked in a simulation only; test 6c shows whether your sensor behaves that way.
  • Top area: the light sensor looks up, so the direction comes only from the robot shading it while turning. That works when the beam is pointed at the robot (your test). If the flashlight points away from where the line ends, the first turn may see no direction: the robot then drives 12 cm on and tries once more before giving up with error 10.
  • The bell is not detected. The mBot cannot hear it; the robot stops on the measured wall distance. Whether it touches the bell depends on BELL_GAP_CM and how far the bell hangs in front of the wall. The last few centimetres are driven blind, so expect about ±1 cm.
